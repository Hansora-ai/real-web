A practical AI marketing workflow starts with an approved message and a reference visual, then adds controlled motion, audio, and reviewed captions. Generate one stage at a time so a flawed product image does not become the foundation for several expensive video variants.

This Hansora-published guide uses a fictional small business selling ceramic mugs. The example is a production exercise, not a customer result or a promise of campaign performance. Adapt the sequence to the tools and model settings currently available in your account.

1. Define the deliverable before generating

Write a one-paragraph brief: a vertical social teaser showing a blue ceramic mug on a breakfast table, followed by a short product message and a call to visit the store. Specify the real product features you can substantiate. Avoid inventing claims such as dishwasher safety, country of origin, or a discount that the business has not approved.

Set the intended placement, aspect ratio, approximate running time, and review owner. Prepare a small asset folder containing authorized product photographs, approved copy, logo files, and the campaign brief. Decide what success means visually: the handle stays intact, the color matches the reference, and the final text is readable on a phone.

2. Create and approve the visual

Open Hansora's image and video workspace and select an image model that supports the inputs you need. If product fidelity matters, check reference-image support before starting. A text-only request can produce an appealing generic mug without reproducing the product you sell.

Create a vertical lifestyle concept featuring the supplied blue ceramic mug on a pale breakfast table. Soft window light, natural shadows, simple background, room above the mug for text. Preserve the product shape and color. Do not invent lettering or additional products.

Inspect the output against the source photograph. Check the handle, rim, scale, color, and any brand details. If the visual misrepresents the product, revise it or keep the original photo as the hero asset. The purpose is a useful marketing visual, not replacing an accurate product image at any cost.

Keep important text editable

Plan to add exact prices, offer dates, and legal copy during finishing. Even when generated lettering looks convincing, verify every character. Keeping essential copy separate makes later corrections and localization easier and reduces the chance of publishing an inaccurate claim embedded inside a visual.

3. Animate one approved scene

Select a supported image-to-video model and inspect its duration, resolution, and reference requirements. Ask for a restrained movement: a slow push toward the mug with a stable table and background. Complex pouring, hand interaction, or rapid camera changes introduce more elements to inspect.

Generate a short test, watch the entire clip, and review suspicious frames. Look for shape drift, a changing logo, flicker, and impossible reflections. If the product changes during motion, simplify the movement or use a conventional pan over the original image. Our AI video comparison guide explains how to account for retries and usable-shot cost.

4. Prepare narration or music

Use the Hansora audio workspace for a supported voice or music task. Write the spoken script first. A simple line such as “A quiet start, in your favorite mug” is easier to time than a paragraph of product information. Listen for pronunciation, unnatural pauses, and whether the delivery suits the brand.

If using music, ask for a mood that supports the scene and leave space for speech. Review the applicable provider terms for the generated track and intended distribution. Do not assume that generating music automatically clears every possible right or platform requirement. When using a person's voice or likeness, secure appropriate permission rather than suggesting an endorsement they never gave.

5. Assemble and check captions

Bring approved assets into Caption & Montage for the supported caption and assembly steps. Check the available import and export controls; use your usual editor for any timing, mixing, or layout task the current tool does not provide. This workflow does not assume a single automatic render combines every stage.

Review captions word by word against the audio. Correct product names, punctuation, and timing. Use readable contrast and keep important copy away from areas covered by the destination app's controls. Watch once with sound muted to confirm the message still makes sense, then listen again to check narration and music balance.

A small approval checklist

Before making variants, ask one reviewer to sign off on product fidelity, the factual message, rights to the references, spoken copy, caption timing, and the downloaded file. Record the approved version and the changes still needed. Use our campaign production brief to keep those decisions and a stage-by-stage spending log together.

6. Make variants from an approved master

Once the master passes review, create a small number of deliberate variants. Change the opening line, crop, or call to action while preserving the approved product image and factual message. Name files clearly so a discarded draft cannot be mistaken for the final ad.

Hansora's Henshin transformation workflows may be useful for a suitable creative concept, but confirm current requirements and inspect the result carefully. A transformation is optional; it should serve the message and preserve any product characteristics that viewers rely on.

7. Review rights, accuracy, and the final export

Read Hansora's terms and the selected provider's rules before commercial use. Permission to use a service's output does not establish exclusivity, copyright protection, or clearance of third-party material. Check submitted references, logos, music, people, and any claims against the permissions and evidence you hold.

Inspect the downloaded file on a phone and desktop. Check for missing frames, clipped captions, incorrect offers, and poor audio. Confirm the destination's current requirements, including any applicable synthetic-media disclosure. Keep the final copy, reference permissions, selected settings, and approved export with the project record.

Frequently asked questions

Can this workflow run entirely automatically?

Some individual tasks can be requested through connected tools, but this guide uses explicit review between stages. Check current tool support rather than assuming automatic assembly and publishing.

Should AI replace my product photographs?

Use original photography when a generated version fails to represent the product accurately. AI can help with concepts and backgrounds while the real product remains the reference for approval.

Can I publish generated music and voices commercially?

Review the selected provider's terms, reference permissions, and intended distribution. Do not infer complete rights clearance from a successful generation alone.
